Nick Mastronardi grew up in Fayetteville, Georgia. Notre Dame ROTC. About ten years in the Air Force: a weapons lab, teaching at the Academy, a PhD in economics.

At Amazon he worked on AI automated pricing. He served at the White House Council of Economic Advisers while still on active duty. The through-line, as he tells it: public goods lack the competition that punishes a bad private-sector decision. Taxpayers can challenge what a city did — and still have almost no data to stand on. So he built Polco.

A later telling (ActionCOACH, 2024) puts the founding call after Amazon: he rang Alex Pedersen, then at Google, and they started Polco. Both versions stay on this page. This draft does not invent a merger, an LOI, or a SAFE.

Jul 15 · the dance floor

Governance and civics, stuck at a middle-school dance.

Nick’s Jul 15 framing: as local news evaporates, the relationship between governments and residents gets more awkward. Trust is down. Officials don’t always know what citizens think. Neither side is coming onto the floor. Polco’s work, as he told it, is slowly luring governments out — about 400 of them — and helping those cities bring verified residents with them.
